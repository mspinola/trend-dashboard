How these are worked out

Equities and commodities: net futures exposure, % of NAV. Bonds: duration years, each bond future's weight times the duration of the bond it delivers, so a book at −7 gains about 7% of NAV when yields rise one point. Notional would rank a fund short 2-year notes above one short the same amount of 10-year bonds. US dollar: the currency futures are foreign currencies against the dollar, so short the euro is long the dollar. Gross: the long and the short futures notional. Collateral: T-bills and cash, as each fund lists them.

Every bar in a row is on one scale across the funds, so a short bar is a small position, not a rounding.

* Uses rounded durations for the non-US bond futures (Bund, Bobl, Gilt, JGB, Canada), so read it to the nearest year.

How this is worked out, and its record

The COT report shows positions at a Tuesday close. Each fund column is what that fund has bought or sold since then, % of NAV, with price moves left out. Price is the market's own move over the same days. A market gets a call when AHLT or KMLM traded a point or more in it and the market is in a class where that fund's position changes have run with the large speculators' (currencies and commodities, as a rule). "Funds split" means the two traded opposite ways, and no call is made.

Confidence. How often calls like this one were right when the same rule is replayed over every past report week (AHLT 150 weeks, KMLM 97). All calls together: 70%. It depends on what the market's price did over the same days, and on the size of the fund's trade. Four dots: price moved the way the fund traded and the fund traded 4% of NAV or more, right 85% of the time. Three dots: price agreed with a smaller trade, 74%. Two dots: price was flat (under half a percent), 67%. One dot: price moved against the fund, 54%, which the record cannot tell from a coin flip. Blank: no price series, or the funds split. Hover on the dots for the figure.

These are past frequencies from one stretch of history, good to within about five points, and they say nothing about the size of the move. The four-dot grade is the newest cut and rests on the fewest calls (206).

Commercials. The Commercials column (on a phone, the small line under each call) is the commercials' implied move, the large-spec call flipped. Commercials are mostly the other side of the large speculators' trades: their weekly net changes have gone the opposite way about 9 weeks in 10. Graded against the commercials' own moves, the flipped call has been right as often as the call itself (72% against 70% overall, and within a few points at every confidence grade), so the same dots apply.

Price only. A commodity with no fund call is read from its price move alone: large speculators tend to buy what is rising. The dots are on the same scale and stop at three: three for a move over 4% (right 76% of the time), two for 2 to 4% (72%), one for 1 to 2% (64%). Moves under 1% are left out (close to a coin flip), and so are currencies, equity indexes and rates, where price alone says little or nothing. A fund's trade shown on such a row did not count: DBMF never does, and AHLT or KMLM only where their trades have run with the large speculators'.

These are past frequencies from one stretch of history, good to within about five points, and they say nothing about the size of the move. A larger fund trade has done better than a smaller one (78% for trades over 4% of NAV), which the dots do not include.
